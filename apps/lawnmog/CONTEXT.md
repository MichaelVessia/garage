# Lawnmog

Lawnmog is a shared first-person lawn care game on Clover Lane. A Block holds all lots on the street. Each lot has a stable address, a lawn, and either a bot or a player. When a player claims the last bot lot, the Block adds two bot lots at one end of the street. New pairs alternate between ends. Karen’s house, The Standards Committee, occupies the center of the street. A resident can claim the home opposite her at 107 Clover Lane.

Each lawn contains patches. Moisture, fertility, grass height, disease, and mow patterns affect curb appeal. A player works on patches with an equipped tool. A patch earns coins and XP once per group of 16 different improved patches. Effective care can still use supplies on a patch that already paid. The Block records gross earnings, supply costs, and any lawn job bonus in the work receipt. The reel mower and watering can give each player a way to work without supply costs. A lawn job counts different patches mowed in one quarter of the lawn. Completing the quarter pays a bonus and starts a job in the next quarter. Players spend coins on equipment. The tool catalog defines purchase prices, prerequisites, and tool descriptions. XP unlocks mow patterns. Time changes lawn condition and lets bots tend their lawns.

The Block transition applies elapsed time and resolves claims, work, purchases, and equipment and pattern selection. A player can put away the equipped tool and walk with empty hands. The Block saves the selected tool or empty hands, selected pattern, automatic watering setting, and current job. Automatic watering uses coins on dry ticks when the system runs. A Cloudflare Durable Object owns the shared state. It identifies a player by a private browser credential and returns a public view that identifies that player's lot. The browser renders the street, sends work commands, and polls for Block updates. A separate presence endpoint exchanges player positions and facing directions for nearby avatars.

The HOA publishes a block standard and an inspection time. Each lot has a current reading and a separate result from its last inspection. Residents can see their next possible fine and the amount paid at the last inspection.

At the end of each game year Karen awards the gold Yard of the Week sign to the resident with the highest curb appeal. The lower address wins a tie. The block cycles through autumn, winter, spring, and summer. Each season lasts three ticks. Lawn patches track weeds and leaves. Mowers cut weeds and the rake bags leaves. A blower sweeps several patches into the resident’s bag. In Push forward mode it moves leaves in the direction the resident faces. A side boundary leads to the next lawn. The front curb leads to the property across the street. A targeted burst sends loose or bagged leaves directly to any other home on the block. Each delivered pile records its sender. A fresh blower Work press on the pile returns its remaining leaves to that sender. The block records a fine and Karen’s anger when leaves land on her lawn.
